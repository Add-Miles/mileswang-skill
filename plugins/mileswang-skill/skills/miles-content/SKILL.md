# Miles Content

Build the piece from verified material, a real scene, and a concrete conflict. Prefer a smaller true idea over a larger attractive claim.

## Lock the mode and truth boundary

Identify the requested mode before editing:

- **Fidelity cleanup:** Preserve the speaker's meaning, order, uncertainty, and voice. Mark unclear transcript material as `[待确认]`.
- **Revision:** Keep the supported core idea while changing structure, density, or expression.
- **Creation:** Build a new piece only from supplied or verified source material.

Collect only what changes the result: target audience and platform, desired audience action, source material, actual scene, central conflict, and claims that require proof. Use the [content brief and deletion checklist](references/content-brief.md) when these inputs are scattered.

Do not invent a personal experience, result, date, revenue, publication record, account performance, quote, or causal claim. Ask one highest-impact question when a missing fact would change the premise. Otherwise narrow the claim or mark the uncertainty.

## Judge the direction directly

State the content promise in one sentence: after consuming this piece, what concrete thing will the audience understand, feel, or do?

- Call out a false, generic, unsupported, or self-indulgent premise directly.
- Identify the exact assumption that fails.
- Replace it with the smallest supported angle that still matters.
- Separate the core goal from optional ideas; do not expand the piece to accommodate every idea.

## Find the scene and conflict

Extract or request:

- **Scene:** who was where, when, doing what;
- **Want:** what the person was trying to achieve;
- **Obstacle:** what prevented or complicated it;
- **Cost or consequence:** what the obstacle changed;
- **Turn:** the observation, decision, or action that moved the situation;
- **Proof boundary:** what the source proves and what it does not.

Open with the strongest relevant fact, action, or conflict already present in the material. Do not manufacture drama.

## Delete before polishing

Remove material that does not carry scene, conflict, evidence, decision, or necessary transition:

- generic trend openings;
- repeated thesis statements;
- process narration that does not help the audience;
- ornamental adjectives and vague intensifiers;
- borrowed authority and unsupported certainty;
- conclusions grander than the evidence;
- default aphorisms, “golden lines,” and forced uplift.

Do not discard an otherwise useful draft for a soft problem such as length, tone, or local wording. Fix soft problems locally. Block delivery for hard errors such as false facts, wrong objects, missing core evidence, privacy exposure, or a broken premise.

## Revise around one spine

Keep one causal spine: scene, conflict, turn, and audience payoff. Preserve concrete nouns, actions, numbers, and constraints when verified. Make every paragraph serve the same promise. Use platform-specific structure only when it improves the real delivery scenario.

For before-and-after evaluation, use the same source and retain an anonymous accepted sample when available. Do not call a rewrite better merely because it is shorter, more fluent, or more dramatic.

## Return the usable result

Default to this compact response:

1. Give a one-sentence verdict if the direction needed correction.
2. Present the revised or created piece.
3. List only the critical removed assumption, unresolved fact, or usage note.

Keep the internal audit out of the final copy. Do not add an inspirational ending or a summary slogan unless the material and delivery purpose require it.

Read [worked revisions and checks](references/examples.md) when the source mixes verified facts with promotional claims or when a concise rewrite needs a concrete comparison.
